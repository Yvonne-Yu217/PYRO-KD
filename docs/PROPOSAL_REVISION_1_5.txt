Cue-Driven Keyframe Selection for Long-Video Audio Description & QA
Revised 6-Week Project Plan | NYU Graduate Computer Vision
Revision notes: this version (1) retargets the project to long videos, (2) replaces the Whisper-only audio stack with a three-stream speech / non-speech / gap design, (3) separates "what is worth describing" from "where it can legally go," (4) adds a trigger-vs-suppress hypothesis for audio events, (5) feeds an audio timeline to the MLLM, and (6) adds explicit pre-commitment checks on datasets and detectors.
1. Project Overview & Scope
Audio Description (AD) narrates key visual events for blind and low-vision (BLV) viewers. Inline AD must fit into natural silences without overlapping dialogue. This project builds and benchmarks a cue-driven keyframe selection and captioning method for long videos that serves two goals: (a) producing well-placed, concise descriptions, and (b) supporting downstream long-video QA.
The core idea is to drive moment selection with both spoken content and non-speech audio (music, applause, honking, laughter, impacts), combined with visual change, at a fixed keyframe budget K. We also test whether prompt-level human priors (AD guidelines and professional exemplars) improve concision and quality.
A central design principle: separate the event cue from the gap constraint.
Salience: what moment is worth describing? (audio events, speech cues, visual novelty)
Feasibility: where can a description legally go? (VAD-defined gaps and word budget)
Benchmarks
Primary evaluation (REFRAMED): movie AD benchmark with dialogue subtitles, scene cuts, and professional AD reference tracks. Measures AD placement recall/precision, speech overlap, and description quality.
Baseline comparison (Cue2Narrate): audio description dataset used to compare against established baselines for automated AD generation and placement. Access and comparability must be verified in Week 1 (see Section 6).
Downstream QA, short form (EgoSchema): 3-minute egocentric QA diagnostic. Only used for audio-aware claims if the distributed clips contain audio (verified in Week 1).
Downstream QA, long form (new): one long-video QA benchmark with an audio track and videos well beyond 5 minutes (candidates: the long split of Video-MME or a comparable benchmark; final choice depends on audio availability and license). This is what supports the long-video claim.
Scope Limits
In scope: videos from 3 minutes up to roughly 30 to 60 minutes for the long-form subset (a small evaluation subset, with hierarchical processing); frozen MLLMs (no fine-tuning); 5 core selectors plus ablation variants; a three-stream audio stack (VAD, Whisper ASR, audio-event tagging); 2 prompt priors; 2 core models plus 1 audio-native ablation model; reference-based, rubric-based, and downstream QA evaluations.
Out of scope: closed captioning for D/HH users; gaze/saliency priors; end-to-end training; BLV human participant studies; legal ADA compliance claims.
2. Audio Stack: Three Streams
The original plan used Whisper for both speech and non-speech audio events. Whisper is only reliable for speech; on music, applause, and noise it tends to hallucinate text, and its bracketed tags (e.g., [MUSIC]) are inconsistent. The audio stack is therefore split into three streams.

3. Cue Scores
3.1 Non-speech event salience (NSE)
Take frame-level class posteriors at roughly a 1 s hop, and drop speech and silence classes.
Detect onsets as sharp rises in a class's probability (music starts, applause begins).
Detect embedding change in CLAP against a running window. This catches transitions with no AudioSet label, such as a genre or tempo shift.
Add cheap salience features: loudness deltas (RMS or LUFS jump) and librosa onset strength.
3.2 Speech cues (SC)
Speaker change (diarization).
Topic or semantic shift in transcript embeddings.
Deictic or visually referential phrases ("look at this," "over there," "watch"), which signal a visual event the dialogue does not fully explain.
Long speech-free stretches, where visual changes need narration.
3.3 Visual novelty (VN)
Camera transitions (PySceneDetect) plus OpenCLIP frame-embedding novelty, measured against a running memory of already-selected moments (not just the neighboring window).
3.4 Gap feasibility (GF)
From Silero VAD: the length of the non-speech gap nearest the candidate, relative to the word budget (Words = floor(Delta_t x 3)).
Overlap penalty (OP) for candidates whose rendered AD would collide with speech.
4. Research Questions & Hypotheses
RQ1 (Selection accuracy and gap fit). At a fixed budget of K moments per video, how do Video-only (V), Audio-only (A), and Fused Audio+Video (AV) selectors compare against Uniform (U) on REFRAMED and Cue2Narrate in (a) agreement with human AD placement and (b) speech overlap?
H1: AV matches V's temporal placement accuracy while matching A's near-zero speech overlap.
RQ2 (Audio event role: trigger vs. suppress). Should non-speech audio events raise or lower a moment's priority?
H2a (Trigger): audio events correlate with visual change, so they raise priority.
H2b (Suppress): events already conveyed by sound are partly redundant for BLV viewers, so visual changes that occur in silence or under unchanging audio deserve higher priority.
Tested via signed and per-class weights on NSE and via an audio-visual disagreement feature (high visual novelty with low audio change). We expect a class-dependent answer (e.g., music onsets may trigger, impulsive sounds like claps may suppress).
RQ3 (Human-prior prompting). Does supplementing plain prompts (P0) with AD guidelines and 3 exemplars (P1) improve rubric scores?
H3: P1 improves objectivity, concision, and gap fit more than overall visual coverage.
RQ4 (Downstream QA utility, short and long). Does audio-aware selection (A/AV), plus an audio-event timeline in the prompt, improve zero-shot multiple-choice QA over visual-only selection (V)?
H4: AV selects keyframes with higher narrative-event density and yields higher QA accuracy, with the largest gain on the long-video benchmark.
RQ5 (Audio context in the MLLM). Does giving the MLLM a compact audio timeline help beyond using audio only for selection? How does this compare to an audio-native model?
H5: the timeline gives a cheap, model-agnostic gain for both captioning and QA.
RQ6 (Baseline and oracle headroom). How far does each deployable selector fall from Cue2Narrate baselines and from an Oracle (O) using human AD timestamps?
5. Experimental Design
5.1 Two-stage selector (core method)
Candidate generation. Propose candidate moments from non-speech audio events, speech cues, and visual novelty.
Gap snapping. Snap each candidate to the nearest valid VAD gap that is long enough for the word budget, with a small lag allowance for events that occur mid-speech.
Selection. Score candidates and choose K with diversity (MMR, or the t_min separation).
AV score:
Score(t) = a*VN(t) + b*NSE(t) + g*SC(t) + d*GF(t) - l*OP(t)
Weights (a, b, g, d, l) are tuned on a dev split. b may be signed or per-class (hand-set or learned per AudioSet superclass) to test RQ2.
5.2 Core factors (20-run matrix preserved)
Selectors (5):
U: uniform timestamps, t_i = i x Duration / (K+1).
V: PySceneDetect transitions plus OpenCLIP novelty.
A: speech cues plus non-speech event salience, snapped to VAD gaps (no visual input).
AV: the full two-stage fused score above.
O: human ground-truth AD timestamps (oracle upper bound).
Priors (2): P0 (plain prompt) vs. P1 (guideline summary plus 3 professional exemplars).
Models (2): one open-weights VLM (Qwen2.5-VL 7B/8B class) and one API model (Gemini Flash or GPT-4o-mini).
Core matrix: 5 selectors x 2 priors x 2 models = 20 runs.
5.3 Ablations (selector-only or single-model, to control cost)
Speech cues only vs. non-speech events only vs. both.
Trigger vs. suppress sign for b (global and per class).
Whisper-only audio events vs. the BEATs/CLAP tagger (justifies the stack change).
Event-time placement vs. snap-to-gap placement.
Audio timeline in prompt: absent vs. present vs. an audio-native model (Qwen2.5-Omni or Qwen2-Audio) that hears the audio directly.
Per-class breakdown: music vs. impulsive sounds (claps, honks) vs. ambient.
Flat vs. hierarchical budget allocation on long videos.
Running novelty memory vs. local-window novelty.
Ablations 1 to 4, 6, 7, and 8 are selector-level (zero or low MLLM cost). Ablation 5 is run on one model with the top-performing selector.
5.4 Prompt audio context
For both captioning and QA, the prompt includes a compact audio timeline, e.g.:
[0:42 music starts] [0:55 applause] [1:03 speech: "..."]
For long videos, the global timeline also serves as a summary for the QA stage.
5.5 Scaling to long videos
Hierarchical chunking: segment by scene, long silence, or music boundaries.
Budget allocation: distribute K across segments in proportion to event density, with a floor so quiet segments are not starved.
Running context memory: embeddings of already-selected moments, so novelty is measured against what has already been described.
5.6 Held-constant controls
Across configurations, the following are identical: K (or K per minute for long videos), minimum temporal separation t_min = 2.0 s, visual window per moment (4 frames across 2 s), the audio timeline format, the word limit per gap (floor(Delta_t x 3)), and temperature (0.0). Whisper is always VAD-gated.

7. Evaluation Suite
A. Selection quality and timing (REFRAMED, Cue2Narrate)
Temporal precision and recall: proportion of selected moments within a window of 2.0 s of human AD timestamps.
Cue2Narrate baseline comparison: against official benchmark scores for placement precision and narrative alignment (subject to verification).
Speech overlap: seconds of collision between rendered AD (at 1.2x speed) and speech detected via Silero VAD and Whisper timestamps.
Silence utilization: ratio of description duration to available gap length.
B. Audio-cue diagnostics (new)
Event detector quality: precision/recall on the hand-labeled subset.
Trigger/suppress analysis: correlation of audio-event onsets with human AD placement, per class; effect of signed b on placement recall.
Silent-visual-event coverage: fraction of human AD moments that occur with no audio change, and how well each selector covers them.
C. Description quality and guideline rubric
Text similarity: OpenCLIP cosine and BERTScore versus professional references.
LLM judge rubric (1 to 5): objectivity, concision/gap fit, non-redundancy, specificity, grounding. Non-redundancy includes not narrating information already conveyed by audio.
D. Downstream utility
Zero-shot QA accuracy (%) on EgoSchema (visual-only unless audio is confirmed) and on the long-form benchmark, with audio-timeline prompt on and off.
Accuracy vs. video length to show whether gains grow on longer videos.
8. Week-by-Week Plan
Week 1: Environment, data ingestion, and verification gate
Set up uv environment (Python 3.12), shared GitHub repository, GPU allocations.
Ingest REFRAMED, Cue2Narrate, EgoSchema, and the chosen long-form QA benchmark.
Run the Section 6 checks (EgoSchema audio, Cue2Narrate access, long-form benchmark, begin hand-labeling).
Build frame loader and audio conversion utilities; stand up Silero VAD and VAD-gated Whisper.
Week 2: Audio event stack, feature caching, and end-to-end skeleton
Run the BEATs/PANNs/AST tagger and CLAP embeddings; cache class posteriors and embeddings.
Cache OpenCLIP frame embeddings, VAD intervals, and Whisper transcripts keyed by content hash.
Finish detector sanity check on the 10 hand-labeled clips.
Implement U with P0 on 10 dev videos; build evaluation v0 (BERTScore plus speech overlap).
Week 3: Selectors, two-stage pipeline, and timing benchmark
Build V, A, and AV (candidate generation, gap snapping, MMR selection); tune weights on the dev split.
Implement trigger/suppress variants and the audio-visual disagreement feature.
Implement hierarchical chunking and running-memory novelty.
Run selector-only evaluation on REFRAMED and Cue2Narrate (RQ1, RQ2, RQ6 baselines; zero MLLM cost) plus selector-level ablations.
Design P1 with exemplars; calibrate the 5-criterion LLM judge; define the audio-timeline prompt format.
Week 4: Full matrix and downstream QA
Execute the 20-configuration grid (open-weights and API models).
Run EgoSchema and long-form QA across selectors, with timeline on/off.
Run the audio-native model ablation on one model with the top selector.
Collect blinded peer pairwise ratings (60 comparisons).
Week 5: Statistics, error analysis, code freeze
Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and 95% bootstrap confidence intervals.
Analyze failure cases: speech collisions, hallucinated audio descriptions, missed silent visual events, tagger false positives, mid-speech event handling.
Freeze codebase and artifacts.
Week 6: Reporting and deliverables
Conference-style report, final figures, slides, and a README enabling one-step table reproduction.
9. Risks, Mitigation & Cut List
Risk 1: Speech collisions from incorrect gap estimation. Mitigation: strict word cap of floor(Delta_t x 3), using VAD speech bounds with Whisper timestamps as a cross-check.
Risk 2: API cost or runtime overruns. Mitigation: cache all intermediates keyed by content hash; enforce a hard spend cap in code.
Risk 3: Non-speech tagger underperforms on movie audio. Mitigation: Week 2 sanity check; restrict to reliable classes; fall back on CLAP embedding change and loudness/onset features.
Risk 4: Audio unavailable in a benchmark. Mitigation: Week 1 gate; use the audio-bearing benchmarks for audio claims and treat the rest as visual-only diagnostics.
Risk 5: Scope growth from long videos and added ablations. Mitigation: most ablations are selector-only; long-form evaluation uses a small subset; hierarchical processing relies on cached features.
Risk 6: Signed or per-class weights overfit the dev split. Mitigation: few parameters (per superclass, not per label), cross-validate on dev, report on held-out test only once.
Ordered cut list (applied if behind schedule)
Drop the audio-native model ablation.
Drop the API model and run the 10 open-weights configurations.
Evaluate P1 only on U and the top selector.
Reduce the long-form QA subset size.
Reduce REFRAMED/Cue2Narrate test subset from 50 to 30 videos.
Shrink the peer pairwise study to 30 items.
Reduce per-class trigger/suppress analysis to music vs. impulsive vs. other.
10. Deliverables
Final written report: the 20-configuration results table; REFRAMED and Cue2Narrate timing and quality breakdowns; trigger-vs-suppress findings; audio-timeline and audio-native ablations; EgoSchema and long-form QA results; qualitative error analysis.
Open-source codebase: modular selectors (visual_selector.py; audio_selector.py with VAD, Whisper, and event-tagger integration; gap-snapping and hierarchical-budget modules), Slurm scripts, and cached feature pipelines.
Reproducible experiment data: raw generations, evaluation scores against Cue2Narrate baselines, the hand-labeled audio-event subset, and anonymized peer preference logs.
